Phase 02 Research & Interviews
Phase 02

Listening before designing

We combined secondary research with primary interviews across three housing contexts — formal ownership, informal settlement, and urban renting.

In-depth interviews
Semi-structured conversations with residents across income groups, exploring daily housing friction, documentation challenges, and emotional experiences.
Secondary data review
Analysis of RERA data, PMAY scheme reports, Hyderabad ward office processes, NoBroker/MagicBricks user flows, and RBI mortgage guidelines.
Field observation
Visits to ward offices, informal settlements in Saidabad, and builder show-homes to understand the physical and procedural realities of housing access.

Cross-cutting insight

"Across all three personas, root causes converge on the same structural failure: systems and policies designed for an imagined citizen — literate, settled, formally employed — that excludes the majority of actual users."

Phase 05 How Might We Statements
Phase 05

Reframing problems as possibilities

We converted each root cause into an opportunity-framed "How Might We" question — a bridge from diagnosis to design.

P1 — Homeowner
How might we help middle-class families quickly understand what homes are actually affordable for their income, instead of overwhelming them with luxury listings?
P2 — Informal resident
How might we make housing schemes accessible for migrants and informal workers who contribute to the city but lack local documents?
P3 — Urban renter
How might we reduce the mismatch between rising urban rents and stagnant salaries for young professionals in Hyderabad?
P2 — Informal resident
How might we ensure every applicant receives the same clear and accurate guidance regardless of which office or staff member they approach?
P1 — Homeowner

P3 — Urban renter

Phase 06 6-3-5 Brainwriting
Phase 06

Structured divergent thinking

The 6-3-5 method — 6 participants, 3 ideas each, 5 rounds of iteration — generated a cascade of solutions, each building on the last. Ideas compounded across rounds.

  • R1
    Seed ideas
    Each participant generates 3 initial ideas independently, without influence from others. Raw, unfiltered responses to the HMW prompts.
  • R2
    First iteration
    Sheets rotate. Participants read existing ideas and build on, combine, or challenge them. Ideas begin acquiring specificity and depth.
  • R3
    Cross-pollination
    Themes from different HMW tracks begin merging. Systemic ideas emerge alongside product-level and policy interventions.
  • R4
    Challenge round
    Participants are invited to counter-argue or stress-test existing ideas — surfacing assumptions and strengthening robust concepts.
  • R5
    Synthesis
    Final round synthesises threads. Concepts like "Migrant Worker Housing Card" and "Affordability Dashboard" emerge in near-complete form.
